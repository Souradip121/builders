# AI Overviews cut clicks by 40%: the data + how to get cited

## The numbers
- **Agarwal & Sen experiment** (working paper, June 2026 draft, not peer reviewed), summarised by RevScope: 1,065 US participants on desktop Chrome for 2 weeks in Jan–Feb 2026. Searches with an AI Overview averaged **0.37 clicks** vs **0.62** without: a 39.8% drop in clicks per search. (That is clicks per search, not "40% less total traffic".)
- **Pew Research Center** (Jul 2025, 900 US adults, real browsing in March 2025): users clicked a result on **8%** of visits with an AI summary vs **15%** without. Only **1%** clicked a link inside the summary.
- **Ahrefs** (Feb 2026, 300k keywords): an estimated **~58%** lower CTR for position 1 when an AI Overview shows (a modelled estimate).
- **DataForSEO** (via RevScope): AI Overviews appeared on 5.93% of results in Sep 2024 and **61.43%** in Aug 2026.

## What Google officially says
From "AI features and your website" (Google Search Central):
1. There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode: the page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet.
2. You don't need new machine-readable files, AI text files or markup.
3. AI Overview and AI Mode traffic is counted in Search Console → Performance → search type "Web".

## llms.txt
A proposal by Jeremy Howard (Sep 2024), open for community input. No search engine has confirmed it uses llms.txt for ranking or citations, and SE Ranking (≈300k domains) found no link between llms.txt and citations. Fine to add for developer docs; don't expect SEO from it.

## A get-cited checklist
1. Keep SEO basics: indexed, snippet-eligible pages that earn links.
2. Search your 10 most important queries in Google AI Overviews and ChatGPT. Write down which sources they cite.
3. Take part where those sources live (communities, review sites, comparison lists) with real answers.
4. Make pages quotable: answer in the first two lines, publish original data, name your sources, show a "last updated" date, one heading per question.
5. Skip keyword stuffing and "AI-only" markup.
6. Judge each page by signups or revenue, not by rank or visits.
